## The mother — player-named

**38. Biology and Health \& Sex Ed teacher.** Homeroom teacher for `son\\\_1`'s class.
Had her first son at 20.

**What she is:** the appetite was always there, fully formed, and never had an outlet
because there was never time. She is not repressed — she is *unexpressed*. She has never
been confused about what she wants, only about whether she's allowed the hours.

**Why:** twenty years of being needed. She went from girl to wife to mother without a
gap. The calendar is the villain of this game, not any person in it.

**Good liar. Bad at lying to herself.** She can tell Eric nothing happened with a
straight face, then lie awake replaying it.

**Becomes a different woman with each person**, driven by the player's tone choices
(bold / teasing / hesitant). By act 3 the tone tally *is* her personality per character.
Payoff: a scene where both sons are present and she has to be both versions at once.

**Looks:** auburn hair, long waves (repo defaults). Outfits in `AppearanceData`.

**Voice:** dry, competent, observational. Says the true thing on purpose and late.
Never gushes. When she's affected, she gets *quieter*, not louder.

## \## Eric — husband, 40 (husband)

**Architect.** Restoring a vintage motorcycle in the hobby room. Weekends only.

**Sympathetic. Never does anything wrong.** No affair, no cruelty, no objective neglect.
He works, he pays for the house, he does the chores she didn't assign. The marriage is
fine. It's just quiet, and she noticed first.

**Still wants her.** Also wants *new experiences* with her and is bad at saying so. He
suggested something once and she brushed past it. He's 40 and aware the spark is gone.

**Affection is wordless:** coffee in the morning, the good mug, fixes things unasked,
remembers her schedule. **His behaviour never changes — her perception of it does.**
Same coffee at rank 1 is background noise. At rank 8 it's unbearable.

**His ladder runs inverse:** it's about *attention*, not attraction. Ranks 1-3 he's not
noticing. Ranks 4-6 he is. Ranks 7-10 he's paying far too much attention.

**When he finds out, his reaction is not "how could you" — it's "what does that say
about us."** That's what makes the complicit ending earned rather than a twist.

**Voice:** tired, warm, plain. Says the right thing four scenes too late.

## \## Miles — younger son, 14 (son\_1)

**Nerd, gamer. In her homeroom and her health class.**

**Theory and no practice.** Has watched thousands of hours of porn, has *opinions*,
has never touched anyone. Nobody in the family knows about the habit until she finds
out mid-game.

**The switch:** she went off-script in class for fifteen seconds — a joke, an aside —
and the abstract knowledge and the real woman eight feet away collided. She's a person.
She has a body. She has a life that has nothing to do with him.

**His route is realisation.** Passive, obsessive, attentive. He doesn't just find her
attractive, he starts *studying* her — what she wears, where she stands, how she holds
a pen, which words she uses when she's tired. Trained on a screen, now with a live subject.

**Why he's good, when it happens:** not talent. Attention. He's been thinking about it
for three years and is petrified of being bad.

**Dinner table:** quiet — but cataloguing. Nobody knows.

**Voice:** over-explains, backtracks, reaches for metaphors. Talks in technical language
when nervous. Goes silent and then says one devastating thing.

## \## Tyler — older son, 18 (son\_2)

**Football. Star of the school. Not in her class — they only overlap at home.**

**Practice and no theory.** Confident, instinctive, competent, shallow in the way of
someone who has never had to try.

**The switch:** she's the only person in his life who isn't reacting to him. Teachers
are pleased, girls are impressed, teammates defer, Eric is proud in a way that's
really about himself. She's just *used to him*. First time he's ever wanted to change
someone's mind.

Under it: the first woman he ever noticed was her. He forgot for a decade, then
remembered in a kitchen, and it's a problem.

**His route is desire.** Active, forward, physical. He works for it for the first time.

**Dinner table:** bragging, teasing, showing off. Needs a move from her and Eric or
he eats the scene.

**Voice:** blunt, short sentences, no hedging. Says the thing. Fumbles when it matters.

## Rex — family dog (dog)

Big scruffy mutt. Enormous, calm, undignified. **Belongs to the family, but has always
preferred her.**

**He is a route.** Beastiality scenes. Also the only creature in the house that is
unambiguously on her side.

**Scene engine:** he notices tension. Under the table when it's bad, leans on whoever's
upset, scratches at the wrong door at the wrong moment. A way to show the player a room
is charged without anyone saying anything.

**Protective of her.** Growls at the wrong moment. Positions himself between her and
whoever is being too much.

## The dynamic

* **Dinner table** is the only room where all four are locked in together and neither
son can do anything. Best scene location in the game.
* **The brothers notice nothing about each other until late.** Acts 1-2 they operate
privately, each thinking he's alone. The collision in act 3 is an ambush for both.
* **The scarcity is the villain.** Not Eric, not the sons. The hours.
* **The classroom** is shared only by her and `son\\\_1`, where she has formal authority.
* **The house at home** is shared only by her and `son\\\_2`, with no institutional framing.
